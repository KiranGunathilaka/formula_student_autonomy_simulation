GPU Lidar Check

Does Gazebo's GPU lidar put the right points on the cones, and does the whole stack drive on it?

5 November 2026 · RTX 5060 Laptop GPU (driver 580.178.04), Intel Raptor Lake iGPU, Ubuntu 22.04 · Docker image falcon_autonomy:latest (ROS 2 Humble, Gazebo 11.10.2) · ADS-DV on small_track (71 cones)

Results

  • Gazebo's lidar agrees with the ray-cast. On a driven lap, for all 8 lidar set-ups, the two detected exactly the same cones (71 of 71). Gazebo's median first-detection range was 0.1–1.1 m shorter, and it put 0.76–0.78× as many points on each cone (median over range bands).
  • The lidar comparison holds. The ray-cast results used to compare lidars match what Gazebo's own sensor sees: the ATX first detects cones at about 30 m (the track's limit), 64 channels at about 23 m, 32 channels at 19–20 m and the VLP-16 at 13 m.
  • The full stack runs on the GPU. Hesai ATX, plate 0.57 m: lap completed, 0 cones touched, fused cones within 15 m a median 5 cm from the true cone; Ouster OS1-32, post 0.70 m: lap completed, 0 cones touched, fused cones within 15 m a median 3 cm from the true cone
  • Render on the NVIDIA GPU. Both Intel GPUs tested (this laptop's Raptor Lake and the 8 GB test PC's UHD 630) render the 120° ATX correctly but put no points on cones past 5 m with the 360° VLP-16. The RTX gets both right. On this laptop OpenGL goes to the Intel GPU unless two PRIME offload variables are set; docker/docker_run.sh now sets them.
  • No ground in Gazebo's cloud. The EUFS ground plane has a collision shape but no visual, and gpu_ray sees only visuals, so the cloud holds cones and the car but no ground. A real lidar, and Gazebo's CPU ray sensor, return the ground.

How the check works

One drive is counted twice. Gazebo's gpu_ray lidar renders the lidar profile's beams on the GPU and publishes the cloud on /lidar/points. At the same time, the cone_coverage node casts the same beams itself, from the car's true pose, against each cone as an exact frustum. Both count the points on every cone in every frame and write a report: <run>/pointcloud/ and <run>/raycast/. coverage_compare puts the two side by side.

  • Same shapes: the ray-cast uses the size of the Gazebo cone meshes (coverage_cone_shape:=eufs: small cones 304 mm tall, 160 mm across the base), so both count points on the same cones. The rulebook FS cones (325 mm, 228 mm across) are bigger, which is why the lidar comparison uses fs.
  • Detected means at least 3 points on the cone in one frame, from at least one channel.
  • Isolated: each container kept ROS on its own machine (ROS_LOCALHOST_ONLY=1). Two simulators on one LAN and ROS domain receive each other's clock, TF and clouds, which makes both counts meaningless.

Run it yourself (and record a video)

  1. On the laptop, in the repo: ./docker/docker_run.sh, then inside source /workspace/docker/build.sh.
  2. Drive one lap with both counters, with Gazebo and RViz open: 
    ros2 launch falcon_bringup sim.launch.py lidar:=hesai_atx coverage:=true \
    coverage_source:=both coverage_cone_shape:=eufs laps:=1
    For another lidar: lidar:=ouster_os1_32 lidar_z:=0.70. For the full stack add cones:=perception.
  3. Record: in a second terminal on the host, scripts/record_screen.sh (q or Ctrl-C stops; the video goes to runs/videos/).
  4. What to look for in RViz: each cone has two markers, the ray-cast's verdict below and Gazebo's above (green detected, orange too few points, red in view but no points, grey never in view). On a sound GPU they turn green together as the car drives, and the lidar points sit on the cones.
  5. Compare: ros2 run falcon_evaluation coverage_compare ~/falcon_runs/<run>. The reports are in runs/ on the host.
  6. Perception: with cones:=perception, open a second container shell (./docker/docker_run.sh again) and run ros2 run falcon_evaluation perception_check --seconds 60 while the car drives.
  7. All eight set-ups: ros2 run falcon_evaluation lidar_sweep $(ros2 pkg prefix falcon_evaluation)/share/falcon_evaluation/config/sweep_gpu_lidar.yaml, then coverage_compare ~/falcon_runs/<sweep>. About 1 minute per set-up on this laptop.
  8. Check which GPU Gazebo renders on: grep -o 'libGLX_[a-z]*' /proc/$(pgrep -f ^gzserver)/maps | sort -u inside the container (libGLX_nvidia = RTX).

Limits

  • Gazebo adds Gaussian range noise (σ 2 cm, from the lidar profile) but no dropouts, intensity, rain or dust, and its cloud has no ground, so the lidar detector's ground filter is not exercised.
  • Gazebo put 22–24 % fewer points on each cone than the ray-cast; the detected cones and the reach barely changed.
  • One track (small_track, 102 m), dry, flat ground, one lap per set-up.
